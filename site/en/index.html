Deterministic tools about the Romanian state, running entirely in the browser. Some model a proposal that has not happened yet; others read what has already happened. The difference matters, so the pages are kept apart.

Eight regions, not 41

Romania has had eight development regions since 2004 and uses them only for statistics and EU funds. The state instead keeps an office in nearly every county for every function. The figures come from official registers and can be checked in the simulators.

41 → 8counties, merged into the eight development regions

Simulators

What would happen if. Each takes a published proposal, models what it actually says, and lets you argue with it. No number here is a measurement. The simulators are in Romanian.

  • Administrative reform
    Merging Romania's 3,186 local authorities (UATs). An interactive map with adjustable thresholds, and every unit with the reason it looks the way it does.
    Data: INS, 2021 census
  • Public-sector pay
    The pay grid against what is actually paid, with the source of every figure.
    Data: MMFTSS, 20.08.2026
  • Public transport
    How long it would take to reach your county seat by bus if every UAT had a timetable, and what that costs a year compared with the savings the merger claims.
    Data: OpenStreetMap, 2026
  • Justice reform
    241 courts, the caseload of each and the load per judge, and what merging them into 42 tribunals and 15 courts of appeal would mean.
    Data: CSM, 2025
  • Land tax
    What land is worth by the notaries' price guides, what is taxed per hectare today, and which rate on value would raise exactly the same money.
    Data: Fiscal Code, 2026
  • Deconcentrated services
    The state's 919 territorial offices, from 41 counties into eight development regions: 820 offices that could be regionalised into 179, family by family, on the same eight regions as the justice reform.
    Data: ANFP, 2025
  • State companies
    1,247 companies by activity: network utilities, from 659 entities to 56 regional operators, and who stays national, local or out of reach.
    Data: Ministry of Finance, 2019–2024

Monitors

What exists, in fact. These model nothing: they read the public record and make it searchable, with the source of every row.

  • Legislation linter
    What the law in force says about a draft act: missed legal deadlines, terms that changed along the way and candidate contradictions, each finding with the article it comes from.
  • Public procurement
    Unit prices paid by Romanian public institutions, comparable across authorities and searchable in the browser. OCDS format, open data.
Tools for public debate, not entitlement calculators. The simulators compute what a document says, not what anyone receives. The monitors report what was published, not what is right. Where the data runs out, the page says so. · Code and methodology · Privacy · Română
